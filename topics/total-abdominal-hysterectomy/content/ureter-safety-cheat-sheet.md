# TAH Ureter Safety & Injury Management Guide
### *Resident Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet*

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Golden Rule:** Know where the ureter is BEFORE clamping, cutting, or tying.

---

## 🎯 Anatomy & The 4 Danger Zones

### Step 1–4: Early Exposure & Bladder Flap
* **Round Ligament:** Far from ureter. *Minimal risk.*
* **Broad Ligament:** Ureter on posterior leaf. Palpate as a firm "guitar string."
* **Bladder Flap:** Avoid blind force. Ureter is lateral/posterior to dissection plane.

---

### Danger Zone 1: Infundibulopelvic (IP) Ligament *(High Risk)*
* **Location:** Ureter runs *immediately beneath* ovarian vessels at pelvic brim.
* **Safe Technique:** Visualize or palpate the ureter crossing the common/external iliac bifurcation before closing the clamp.

---

### Danger Zone 2: Uterine Artery Crossing *(Very High Risk)*
* **The "Bridge":** Uterine artery is superior; Ureter is inferior (*"Water under the bridge"*).
* **Pro-Tip:** Pull uterus strongly to the *opposite* side to increase the physical distance between the artery and ureter.
* **The "Push-Down":** After clamping and cutting the uterine artery, push the ligated stump downward 1.5–2 cm along the cervix.

---

### Danger Zone 3: Cardinal Ligament *(Highest Risk)*
* **Warning:** The anatomical distance between cervix and ureter can be mere millimeters.
* **Rule:** Stay tightly against the physical contour of the cervix. Lateral bites = Ureter injury. Maintain the *"Roundness of the Cervix"* cutting arc.

---

### Danger Zone 4: Vaginal Cuff Angles *(High Risk)*
* **Risk:** Suture ligation at the lateral vaginal angles catching the ureteral entry into the bladder.
* **Safety:** If a stitch feels like it is grabbing "too much tissue," stop, unclamp, and reassess anatomy.

---

## 🚨 Suspected Ureteral Injury Protocol

### ⚠️ Intraoperative Warning Signs
* Visualizing direct transection or partial laceration.
* Unexplained fluid pooling in the pelvis (urine smell, straw color, or non-clotting fluid).
* Dusky, devitalized, or dilated segment of the ureter.
* A suture or clamp clearly visible traversing the course of the ureter.

---

### 🛠️ Immediate Action Steps
1. **Stop and Call for Help:** Notify attending surgeon and consult Urology or Gynecologic Oncology immediately.
2. **Do Not Cut Blindly:** If a suture or clamp is suspected to be on the ureter, undo/remove it gently rather than cutting tissue to free it.
3. **Assess Perfusion:** Look for the longitudinal "striping" pattern of the ureter's adventitial microvasculature. Watch for active peristalsis (gently tap it with forceps to induce a peristaltic wave).
4. **IV Fluorescein / Methylene Blue / Indigo Carmine:** Administer IV dye (or perform a rigid cystoscopy) to check for pelvic extravasation or confirm bilateral ureteral jet patency.
5. **Prepare for Repair/Stenting:**
   * *Crush / Thermal Injury:* Requires ureteral stenting (JJ stent) for 4–6 weeks.
   * *Distal Transection (<4–5 cm from bladder):* Ureteroneocystostomy (ureteral reimplantation with psoas hitch).
   * *Mid/Upper Transection:* Tension-free primary ureteroureterostomy over a JJ stent with spatulated, absorbable sutures.
