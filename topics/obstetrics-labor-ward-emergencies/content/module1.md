## 🎯 Clinical Learning Objectives
* Indications: Restrictive vs routine episiotomy; indicated in fetal distress, rigid perineum, operative vaginal delivery, and shoulder dystocia risk.
* Mediolateral episiotomy: Angled at 45 to 60 degrees from the posterior fourchette towards the ischial tuberosity during crowning.
* Three-layer anatomic repair: Vaginal mucosa (continuous locking 2-0 Vicryl), perineal muscles, and subcuticular skin closure.

---

#### Core Clinical Takeaways: Episiotomy & Delivery
* **Selective vs Routine Episiotomy:** Evidence (ACOG/RCOG/WHO) strongly recommends **selective/restrictive episiotomy** rather than routine incision. Routine use increases 3rd/4th-degree perineal tears (OASIS) without maternal or fetal benefit.
* **Incision Technique (Mediolateral):**
  * **Timing:** Performed only when the head is crowning and stretching the perineum (distending 3–4 cm during a contraction).
  * **Angle:** Initiated at posterior fourchette and angled strictly at **45° to 60°** towards the ischial tuberosity (never median/midline in Asian/Indian anatomy due to short perineal body).
* **Stepwise Anatomic Closure:**
  1. **Apex anchoring:** Place first suture 1 cm above the vaginal apex to secure retracted vessels.
  2. **Vaginal mucosa:** Continuous locking 2-0 polyglactin 910 (Vicryl) to the hymenal ring.
  3. **Perineal muscle layer:** Reapproximate bulbospongiosus and transverse perineal muscles.
  4. **Subcuticular skin closure:** Continuous intradermal suture without cutaneous knots to minimize postpartum perineal pain.
